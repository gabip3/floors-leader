How Long Does a Kitchen Remodel Really Take?

A kitchen can look unchanged for several days, then suddenly become the busiest room in the house - just without a sink, range, or usable countertops. When homeowners ask how long a kitchen remodel will take, the honest answer depends on the scope, the condition behind the walls, and how many decisions are made before demolition begins.

For most professionally managed projects, a kitchen remodel takes 6 to 12 weeks of on-site construction after materials are selected, ordered, and delivered. A light update may take two to four weeks. A full kitchen renovation involving new cabinets, flooring, layout changes, electrical work, or plumbing can take three months or longer from planning through final completion.

How Long Does a Kitchen Remodel Take by Scope?

The size of the project has the biggest effect on the calendar. Replacing visible finishes is much different from rebuilding the room around a new layout.

Cosmetic kitchen updates: 2 to 4 weeks

A cosmetic update usually keeps the existing floor plan and major utilities in place. It may include painting, a new backsplash, new light fixtures, countertop replacement, cabinet hardware, and flooring. If cabinets are in good condition, refinishing or painting them can also fit this category.

These projects move faster because there is less demolition and fewer inspections. Still, countertop fabrication can add time. Stone countertops are generally measured after base cabinets are installed, then fabricated before final installation. That step alone may take one to three weeks, depending on the material and fabricator schedule.

Mid-range kitchen remodels: 6 to 10 weeks

A mid-range remodel often includes new cabinets, counters, flooring, appliances, lighting, backsplash, and paint. The kitchen layout may stay mostly the same, but old materials come out and the room is rebuilt in stages.

Cabinet installation sets the pace. Once cabinets are secured and level, countertop measurements can happen. After countertops are installed, the team can complete the backsplash, reconnect plumbing fixtures, and finish appliance installation. This sequence is why a kitchen can feel close to done for a week or two before the final details come together.

Full kitchen renovations: 10 to 16 weeks or more

A full renovation may involve removing walls, relocating the sink or range, adding an island, upgrading electrical service, moving gas lines, repairing framing, or opening the kitchen to a living area. These jobs require more coordination and can involve permits and inspections.

A larger kitchen project also affects nearby spaces. New hardwood flooring, for example, may need to extend into adjoining rooms to create a consistent finish. That can add installation and finishing time, but it often produces a better result than stopping new flooring at the kitchen threshold.

The Kitchen Remodel Timeline, Phase by Phase

Construction time is only one part of the overall schedule. Homeowners should allow time before demolition for design decisions, material ordering, and permit preparation.

Planning and selections: 2 to 8 weeks

This is where a realistic schedule starts. Measurements, budget decisions, cabinet style, appliance sizes, flooring, countertop material, fixtures, and lighting all need to be coordinated. Appliance specifications matter early because they determine cabinet openings, electrical requirements, ventilation, and plumbing locations.

Custom cabinets can require considerably more lead time than stock options. Specialty tile, imported fixtures, and made-to-order range hoods can do the same. Ordering early helps prevent a finished room from waiting on one missing part.

Permits and scheduling: 1 to 4 weeks

Not every kitchen update requires a permit, but structural, plumbing, electrical, and gas changes often do. Permit timing varies by municipality and by project complexity. In the Atlanta metro area, homeowners should plan for local requirements rather than assuming every jurisdiction follows the same process.

A contractor can schedule labor more reliably once permit needs and material arrival dates are clear. Starting demolition before key products are available may create a longer period without a functioning kitchen.

Demolition and rough work: 1 to 3 weeks

During demolition, the team removes existing cabinets, old flooring, wall finishes, and sometimes soffits or non-load-bearing walls. This is also when hidden conditions can appear: water damage under a sink, outdated wiring, uneven subfloors, or plumbing that does not meet current code.

After demolition comes rough plumbing, electrical, HVAC adjustments, framing, and any structural work. Inspections may occur before walls are closed. This stage is not always visually exciting, but it is where safe, durable work is established.

Walls, floors, and cabinets: 2 to 4 weeks

Once rough work passes inspection, drywall repairs, wall preparation, flooring, and cabinet installation can begin. The exact order depends on the flooring type and cabinet plan. In many kitchens, cabinets are installed before floating floors. Hardwood and tile require different installation approaches and must be planned around transitions, appliances, and finished floor height.

Cabinets need careful fitting, especially in older homes where walls and floors may not be perfectly square. Good installation is not just about getting boxes on the wall. It affects countertop fit, door alignment, appliance clearance, and the finished appearance of the entire room.

Countertops, finishes, and final work: 2 to 4 weeks

After cabinet installation, countertop templates are taken for fabrication. When the countertops are installed, the project moves into its finishing stage: backsplash tile, paint touch-ups, trim, sink and faucet connections, lighting fixtures, appliance hookups, and final adjustments.

The last 10 percent of a kitchen remodel can take longer than homeowners expect because several trades return for smaller tasks. That is normal. A proper final walkthrough should address cabinet adjustments, caulking, grout, hardware alignment, flooring transitions, and any remaining punch-list items.

What Can Delay a Kitchen Remodel?

Not every delay is avoidable, but many are manageable with early planning. Material availability is a common issue, particularly for cabinets, countertops, tile, and appliances. A damaged cabinet panel or delayed refrigerator can hold up the final stage even when most of the room is complete.

Hidden conditions are another factor. Older homes may have subfloor damage, water intrusion, insufficient electrical capacity, or previous work that needs correction. These discoveries can change both cost and schedule, but addressing them properly protects the investment in the new kitchen.

Changes after construction begins also extend timelines. Moving the island, switching countertop materials, changing appliance models, or adding lighting after walls are open can require new materials and revised trade schedules. Finalizing major choices before demolition keeps the project moving.

How to Keep the Project Moving

The best way to shorten a kitchen remodel is not to rush the work. It is to reduce avoidable waiting. Select appliances before cabinets are ordered, approve materials before demolition, and make sure the contractor has access to the home and a clear area for staging materials.

Homeowners should also plan for daily life without a full kitchen. Set up a temporary station with a microwave, coffee maker, refrigerator access, and disposable or easy-to-wash dishware. If the project includes hardwood flooring or work in nearby rooms, move furniture and valuables before the crew arrives so the installation area is ready.

Working with one contractor for connected improvements can simplify coordination. A kitchen remodel is temporary disruption with a lasting payoff. Give the work enough time for correct preparation, quality installation, and final details, and you will be far more likely to end up with a kitchen that works as well as it looks.
