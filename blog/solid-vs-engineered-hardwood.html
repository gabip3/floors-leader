Solid vs. engineered hardwood: which is right for your home?

If you're putting hardwood in an Atlanta home, you'll hear two words over and over: solid and engineered. They look nearly identical once installed, but under the surface they behave differently, and the right pick depends on your subfloor, your budget, and how you live.

What's the actual difference?

Solid hardwood is exactly that: one solid piece of wood, top to bottom. Engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer on top bonded to layers of plywood underneath. That layered core makes engineered boards more stable when humidity swings, which matters a lot in Georgia summers.

When solid hardwood wins

Solid floors can be sanded and refinished many times over decades, so they're the long-game choice for main floors you plan to keep. If your home is on a raised foundation with a wood subfloor, solid is usually a natural fit.

When engineered makes more sense

Over concrete slabs, basements, or rooms with big temperature changes, engineered boards hold their shape better and can often be glued down directly. You still get a genuine wood surface, just with fewer worries about gapping or cupping.

The honest bottom line
